## Morphological and genomic characterization of a novel *Hepatozoon* lineage in the critically endangered insular pitviper *Bothrops sazimai* (Squamata: Serpentes: Viperidae)

Igor Salles de Oliveira<sup>1,3</sup>, Giselle Pessanha Pessoa<sup>1</sup>, Giovanna Yumi Scorsim Omura<sup>5</sup>, Denis Jacob Machado<sup>5</sup>, Milton Yutaka Nishiyama Junior<sup>2</sup>, Maria José de Jesus Silva<sup>1,3</sup>

<sup>1</sup>Laboratório de Ecologia e Evolução, Instituto Butantan; <sup>2</sup>Laboratório de Toxinologia Aplicada, Instituto Butantan; <sup>3</sup>Instituto de Biociências, Universidade de São Paulo; <sup>4</sup>Instituto de Ciências Biomédicas, Universidade de São Paulo; <sup>5</sup>Department of Bioinformatics and Genomics, University of North Carolina at Charlotte

Animal welfare of captive species is an important factor in conducting suitable conservation efforts, and parasitological screening helps anticipate health issues and clarify host-parasite evolution. *Hepatozoon* (Hemogregarinidae) is one of the predominant blood parasites of snakes reported. Recent descriptions in South America suggest the presence of cryptic diversity not recognized previously with morphology alone. *Bothrops sazimai* is a critically endangered pitviper endemic to a Brazilian island isolated by Pleistocene/Holocene sea-level oscillations, with a captive population kept at Instituto Butantan for conservation purposes. Herein, we report the first record of *Hepatozoon sp*. in *B. sazimai*, found incidentally during blood smear screening and its host genome assemble, characterizing its nuclear 18S rRNA profile and complete mitochondrial and apicoplast genomes to resolve its evolutionary and taxonomic status. Giemsa-stained blood smears from a captive female were scanned (Zeiss AxioScan Z1) and analysed in QuPath for morphometrics. Genomic DNA from the same sample was sequenced on a PromethION (Nanopore) and assembled with Flye. Organellar genomes were annotated in Geneious, tRNAscan-SE and RFAM, and a Maximum-Likelihood 18S rRNA phylogeny was built in IQ-TREE. A single gamont morphotype was recovered (length 11.9+/-1.3; width 4.3+/-0.7; nucleus length 5.0+/-0.9; nucleus width 2.4+/-0.6 micrometers). Assembly recovered a unique mitochondrial and apicoplast genomes alongside two divergent intra-individual 18S variants (p-distance 1.7%). The dominant variant (525x coverage) was recovered outside the *H. cevapii* clade (75% bootstrap; >1.8% divergence), while the minor variant (155x) nested within it (70%; 0.6-1.3% divergence). The mitogenome was a circular molecule of 10,479 bp, longer than the comparable amphibian-associated *Hepatozoon* references (for example, *H. catesbianae*, 6.3 kbp), with no segmental duplications but several fragmented repeats in intergenic regions. The apicoplast genome (32,316 bp; 22 protein-coding genes, 27 tRNA, 4 rRNA) closely matched the only other *Hepatozoon* apicoplast described (*H. canis*, 31.9 kbp). Based on our results, we suggest the presence of a distinct *Hepatozoon* lineage in this insular host, uncovering structural plasticity of apicomplexan organellar genomes for this genus. Moreover, regarding the 18S variants, we suggest ongoing incomplete concerted evolution of rDNA copies, possibly driven by the island isolation.
