## Morphological and genomic characterization of a novel *Hepatozoon* lineage in the critically endangered insular pitviper *Bothrops sazimai* (Squamata: Serpentes: Viperidae)

Giselle Pessanha Pessoa<sup>1,4</sup>, Igor Salles de Oliveira<sup>1,3</sup>, Giovanna Yumi Scorsim Omura<sup>5</sup>, Denis Jacob Machado<sup>5,6</sup>, Milton Yutaka Nishiyama Junior<sup>2</sup>, and Maria José de Jesus Silva<sup>1,3</sup>

<sup>1</sup>Laboratório de Ecologia e Evolução, Instituto Butantan, São Paulo, Brazil. <sup>2</sup>Laboratório de Toxinologia Aplicada, Instituto Butantan, São Paulo, Brazil. <sup>3</sup>Instituto de Biociências, Universidade de São Paulo, São Paulo, Brazil. <sup>4</sup>Instituto de Ciências Biomédicas, Universidade de São Paulo, São Paulo, Brazil. <sup>5</sup>Department of Bioinformatics and Genomics, University of North Carolina at Charlotte, Charlotte, NC, USA. <sup>6</sup>Center for Computational Intelligence for Predicting Health and Environmental Risks (CIPHER), University of North Carolina at Charlotte, Charlotte, NC, USA.

Parasite screening of captive animals protects conservation programs and shows how parasites evolve with their hosts. *Hepatozoon* (Apicomplexa: Adeleorina: Hepatozoidae) is the blood parasite most often reported in snakes. Recent studies in South America keep finding lineages that gamont morphology alone cannot separate. *Bothrops sazimai* lives only on Ilha dos Franceses, Espírito Santo, Brazil, and its describers proposed it as Critically Endangered. Instituto Butantan keeps a captive colony for conservation. Here we report *Hepatozoon* sp. in *B. sazimai* for the first time. We found it in blood smears and in the long-read data used to assemble the host genome. We scanned Giemsa-stained smears of an adult female (Zeiss AxioScan Z1) and measured gamonts in QuPath. We sequenced DNA from the same blood on an Oxford Nanopore PromethION, assembled it with Flye, and recovered the parasite reads from the host assembly. We annotated the organellar genomes with Geneious, MITOS2, tRNAscan-SE, and Rfam, and placed the 18S rRNA variants in a maximum likelihood tree of 150 adeleorin sequences built with IQ-TREE. Gamonts measured 11.9 ± 1.3 µm by 4.3 ± 0.7 µm, with a nucleus of 5.0 ± 0.9 µm by 2.4 ± 0.6 µm. No compared species combines such a short, broad gamont with such a long, narrow nucleus. The host carries two 18S rRNA variants. The dominant variant (V1, 944 reads) falls outside a clade of Neotropical snake parasites. The minor variant (V2, 271 reads) falls inside that clade, next to *H. annulatum* (SH-aLRT 97.7, UFBoot 100). The mitochondrial genome is a circular molecule of 10,479 bp, 63% longer than the largest published congener (6,413 bp). The extra length lies between *cox1* and *cob*, where *cox3* now follows *cob*, a gene order not seen in other *Hepatozoon*. The apicoplast genome is 32,316 bp, with 22 protein-coding genes, 26 tRNA genes, and 4 rRNA genes, close in size to the only other published *Hepatozoon* apicoplast genome (*H. canis*, 31.9 kb). *Bothrops sazimai* therefore carries a distinct *Hepatozoon* lineage. Its two 18S variants point to divergent rDNA copies within one genome or to a mixed infection.
