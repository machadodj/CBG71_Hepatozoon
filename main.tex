\secaotopo{Introduction}
\begin{minipage}[t]{32.5cm}\vspace{0pt}\justifying
\Hep{} (Apicomplexa: Adeleorina: Hepatozoidae) is the blood parasite most often reported in snakes. Most species were described from gamont shape and short 18S rRNA fragments. Recent studies in South America keep finding lineages that morphology alone cannot separate.

\vspace{0.4cm}
The Laboratório de Ecologia e Evolução (Instituto Butantan) keeps critically endangered snakes for conservation. We found \Hep{} by chance in blood smears of \Bsaz{} prepared for flow cytometry and genome studies. \Bs{} lives only on Ilha dos Franceses, Espírito Santo, Brazil (Fig.~1)\textsuperscript{1}. Rising sea levels cut the island off from the mainland within the last 11,000 to 55,000 years\textsuperscript{2}.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\colorbox{fundo}{\begin{minipage}{\dimexpr\linewidth-2\fboxsep\relax}\justifying
\vspace{0.3cm}{\bfseries\color{evento}Objective.} Characterize a \Hep{} lineage found in \Bs{} by gamont morphology, 18S rRNA, and complete mitochondrial and apicoplast genomes, and compare it with known species.\vspace{0.3cm}
\end{minipage}}
\end{minipage}\hfill

\secao{Methods}
We scanned Giemsa-stained blood smears of an adult female (Zeiss AxioScan Z1), measured gamonts in QuPath, and compared them with 28 records of snake \Hep{} from 14 studies. We pulled parasite reads from the host's Nanopore genome assembly (Fig.~2) and placed the 18S variants in an IQ-TREE maximum likelihood tree of 150 adeleorin sequences.
\figura{Figura_02.pdf}{\textbf{Figure 2.} Workflow: gamont morphometry (top) and parasite genomics (bottom). Created in BioRender (BioRender.com).}

\secao{Results}
\begin{minipage}[t][\altquatro][s]{\dimexpr\coluna-\largquatro-1.5cm\relax}\vspace{0pt}\justifying
{\fontsize{30}{36}\selectfont\bfseries Short gamonts with a long, narrow nucleus\par}\vspace{0.25cm}
Gamonts measured 11.9\,±\,1.3\,µm by 4.3\,±\,0.7\,µm, with a nucleus of 5.0\,±\,0.9\,µm by 2.4\,±\,0.6\,µm (Fig.~3).
\vfill
\begin{minipage}[b]{14cm}\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figure_03.pdf}\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[b]{\dimexpr\linewidth-14.8cm\relax}\legenda\RaggedRight \textbf{Figure 3.} Giemsa-stained blood smear of \Bs{}. Two erythrocytes carry curved \Hep{} gamonts beside the nucleus.\end{minipage}\par
\vfill
Only \textit{H.~atrocis} has shorter gamonts among the compared species (Fig.~4A). The gamont is short and broad (length/width 2.8), but its nucleus is long and narrow (length/width 2.1). No compared species combines the two (Fig.~4B).
\vfill
{\fontsize{30}{36}\selectfont\bfseries Two 18S variants that do not group together\par}\vspace{0.25cm}
The host carries two 18S rRNA variants: V1 is dominant (944 reads) and V2 is minor (271 reads). Both fall inside \Hep{} (Fig.~5). V2 sits in a clade of Neotropical snake parasites (SH-aLRT 97.7, UFBoot 100), next to \textit{H.~annulatum}. V1 falls outside that clade, as sister to a group of snake and turtle parasites.
\end{minipage}\hfill

}\end{minipage}}

\subsecao{A large mitochondrial genome with a new gene order}
The mitochondrial genome is a circular molecule of 10,479\,bp, 63\% longer than the largest published congener (6,413\,bp). It carries \textit{cox1}, \textit{cox3}, \textit{cob}, and 25 rRNA fragments, like its relatives. The extra length sits between \textit{cox1} and \textit{cob}, where \textit{cox3} now follows \textit{cob} (Fig.~7). The apicoplast genome is 32,316\,bp, close to the only other published \Hep{} apicoplast genome (\textit{H.~canis}, 31.9\,kb). It carries 22 protein-coding genes, 26 tRNA genes, and 4 rRNA genes (Fig.~6).
\figura{Figure_06.pdf}{\textbf{Figure 6.} Apicoplast genome, circular but drawn linear. Genes on the + strand sit above the line, genes on the \textminus{} strand below.}
\figura{Figure_07.pdf}{\textbf{Figure 7.} Mitochondrial genomes of \Hep{}. Ribbons link \textit{cox1}, \textit{cox3}, and \textit{cob} between neighboring genomes.}

\secao{Conclusions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \Bs{} carries a \Hep{} lineage that differs from every compared species in gamont shape, 18S rRNA, and mitochondrial genome.
\item Its mitochondrial genome is the largest reported for the genus, with a gene order not seen before.
\item Two 18S variants in one host point to divergent rDNA copies or a mixed infection.
\item Host genome projects also sequence blood parasites, at no extra cost.
\end{itemize}\par\vskip0pt
}\end{minipage}}
